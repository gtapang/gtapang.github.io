01The program

A transdisciplinary research and mentorship program applying data science, complex systems theory, network science, and AI across physical, biological, social, and engineered domains. It facilitates collective mentorship across physics, computer science, molecular biology, information science, and hospitality management, and engages government agencies, industry, and people's organizations as partners.

As a new program, its intended goals are inter-unit collaborations with NIMBB, AIT, IB, MSI, SLIS, and CHE HRIM; scaling graduate mentorship across four thematic areas; and building a sustained pipeline of data science practitioners for public service.

Thrust

Genomics & computational biology

Cancer genomics, bioinformatics, and molecular biology data, co-mentored with NIMBB and MSI.

Thrust · flagship

Tourism data science

Visitor-flow modeling, destination analytics, and policy intelligence for the Department of Tourism.

Thrust

Complexity science

Network science, computational modeling, and topological data analysis across social, transport, and software systems.

Thrust

Bioacoustics

Acoustic monitoring and landscape ecology, with zooarchaeology at the School of Archaeology.

04Six-year impact roadmap

Four thematic groups share computational infrastructure, weekly cross-group seminars, and structured co-mentorship. Grants from DOST, CHED, and industry pay for infrastructure and student support.

  1. 1 August 2026 · Start Four thematic groups stand up on shared computational infrastructure, with weekly cross-group seminars and structured co-mentorship across NIMBB, AIT, IB, MSI, SLIS, and CHE HRIM.
  2. Annual · from Year 1 2–3 publications per year across the four thrusts.
  3. By Year 3 1–2 PhD completions per year, sustained from the Data Science Program cohort.
  4. By Year 4 At least two sustained inter-unit collaborations operating beyond single projects.
  5. By Year 6 DOT-adopted policy outputs from visitor-flow modeling and policy intelligence, serving municipal tourism officers, regional planners, and conservation managers.

05Risks and how they are managed

Student recruitment bottlenecks
Four thematic areas widen the entry points into the program and spread intake across five collaborating units.
Faculty load distribution
Core, associate, and affiliate members share supervision instead of one person carrying it.
Single-adviser dependency
Co-supervision keeps a candidate's progress off a single adviser.
External funding volatility
The program draws on DOST, CHED, and industry. Four thematic areas spread the risk if one line contracts.
